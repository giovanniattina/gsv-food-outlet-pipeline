The corner shops that no registry counts

In informal settlements, much of the food people buy comes from stalls, street vendors and small markets. Official and commercial databases rarely list them. This project finds them in Google Street View, then measures how much each database misses.

The story in four steps

The gap

RAIS, the official register of formal establishments, only records registered shops. A fruit stand in a doorway or a street vendor has no entry, so food access in these areas looks worse than it is.

The idea

Street View already shows these places. A classifier can read the storefront and the facade text, label each place as a healthy or unhealthy food source, and add what the official register misses.

The pipeline

Three stages in sequence. Stage 1 keeps only commercial facades. Stage 2 labels the food source. Stage 3 reads the signage with OCR and fuses it with the image prediction.

The measurement

Detected outlets are compared with RAIS, the official register, to quantify how many food outlets it leaves out.

Part 3 of 6

How the model was chosen

Stage 1 had one job: keep commercial crops and drop the rest. We compared 48 setups across three model families and picked on one priority, commercial recall, because a missed storefront is a missed food outlet.

1 · Which family fits?

Each dot is one setup. Circles were scored on all 558 images with no training. Squares are the supervised model on a held-out set of 112 images (5-seed mean), so the two shapes are not a like-for-like race.

2 · Which prompts help the CLIP models?

Prompt P3 lists concrete urban objects behind each class, then averages them. It beats plain templates (P2) by 10 to 15 points of F1 on every CLIP variant. Binary task, n=558.

Part 4 of 6 · Pipeline stage 3

Reading the signs

Many Brazilian storefronts name what they sell: "Padaria", "Açougue", "Mercadinho". Stage 3 reads that text with OCR, matches it against a food vocabulary and blends the result with the image prediction.

Where text exists, and where it doesn't

Share of food-outlet crops whose facade carries readable text (annotated by hand). Fixed storefronts mostly have a sign. The two classes the study cares about most mostly don't.

OCR cannot see what has no sign. Informal-settlements markets (2 of 9 with text) and street vendors (2 of 12) are the places the databases miss, and the signage branch adds almost nothing for them. The image branch has to carry those classes.

How the fusion rule was compared

Ten ways of combining OCR and image scores, scored on the same 162 food crops with the 11-outlet-type labels. Hover for the setup ID. A plain weighted average at α=0.5 is what the pipeline deploys.

Single run, F1-macro over 11 classes, n=162. Classes are small (9 to 48 crops), so differences of a few points are within noise. Stacking uses 5-fold cross-validation.

What OCR does when it finds nothing

The deployed rule is: no keyword match means the image prediction stands. An earlier variant (Stage 3B) did the opposite and called silent facades others. On a set of 212 crops (162 food plus 50 others), OCR was silent on 58 of them with Jaro-Winkler at 0.85. That variant pushes sign-less vendors into others, and the deployed pipeline does not use it.

Why the weighted average. It was chosen for accuracy: 0.562 for the weighted average at α=0.5 against 0.556 for the OR-gate (n=162, 11 classes). The OR-gate scores slightly higher on F1-macro (0.498 against 0.490), and the two are close.

Part 6 of 6 · The measurement

Street View next to RAIS

The pipeline's output is compared with RAIS, the official register of formal establishments. The two sources describe food outlets in different ways, so this page shows how they differ and how a detection is matched to a record. It does not show the underrepresentation analysis itself.

Two sources, two ways of recording a shop

Both are mapped to the same two labels, healthy and unhealthy, so they can be compared. RAIS gets there from its Locais-Nova groups, Street View from the classifier.

How much each source holds in the study area

Number of records in the favela study area of each city. A Street View record is a panorama where a food outlet was detected, so one shop can appear from neighbouring panoramas. A RAIS record is one registered establishment.

How a detection is matched to a record

A Street View detection counts as overlapping with RAIS when a RAIS record lies within a set distance of the panorama point. Without one, it is Street View only. Pick the distance to see how the split moves; the study uses 50 m.
